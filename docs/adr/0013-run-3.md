# 0013. `run/3`: settings, paid requests, where data went, and what a record has lost

Status: accepted. Changes ADR 0005 (the run record's version and shape), and names ADR
0008 ("What is refused inside a run is in its record"; "What a call and a run may hold")
Date: 2026-11-14
Amended: 2026-11-14, after the review of its first pull request, before it merged.
Changed: an account of one to four characters is a whole account whatever its
characters, and one of seven that ends in three dots is a cut one; a setting's text is
bounded for the reader and by the schema; a number that is not finite, or whose digits
hold a secret, makes no setting; the schema holds a refusal of a request to no action;
the count of each list is looked at before its entries are compared; the record of a
run a dead process left is made only when it is one; and three sentences that were not
exact. Added: what the binary shows of the new lists and of what is lost; what readers
key on.
Amended: 2026-11-14, with task B4b, which this record said would add to it. Added: "A
run's place for facts". Changed: the limit that said no run fills the new lists, and
the sentences that left the executor's part of the sum to that task.
Spec: section 3, "What every run records"; section 6, "Spend"; section 10, "Egress"

## Context

`run/2` was frozen at the tag `m1a` (ADR 0005). Three of its lists could hold nothing,
because their element had no value: `asked.settings`, `spend.requests` and `egress`.
Checkpoint 1B fills them, from three strands that work side by side (the config, the
ledger and the gate), and its plan decides that a run a dead process left is ended with
a small record made only from its admission, which says what it does not know. The plan
allows one version of the record for the checkpoint, defined before the strands that
fill it. This is it.

What the elements are made of was decided first: a quote, a charge and a stage (ADR
0010), a recipient and the classes of data (ADR 0012).

Nothing in the build notes a setting, a paid request or a recipient yet: the strands
do, after this. **This record defines the shapes, their bounds, what reads them, and
the place a run is given for them; until a strand notes something, the executor writes
`run/3` with the three lists empty.**

## Decision

The code is `crates/whizbang-core/src/contract/record.rs`; the schema is
`crates/whizbang-core/schemas/run-3.json`. The tests are in
`crates/whizbang-core/tests/it/contract/`, named below as `record3::…` (documents held
to the types and to the schema), `facts::…` (what is made by the constructors) and
`record::…` (the tests of ADR 0005, which now hold `run/3`).

### What `run/3` is

`run/2`, with these changes and no other. What checkpoint 1A filled of `models`,
`time.durations` and `judge` is as it was: the tests of ADR 0009 pass on `run/3`,
changed only where they read the schema's layout or a field that is now optional
(`asked::…`).

| Where | `run/2` | `run/3` |
|---|---|---|
| `record` | `run/2` | `run/3` |
| `asked.settings` | Holds nothing | A list of settings |
| `spend.requests` | Holds nothing | A list of paid requests |
| `egress` | Holds nothing | A list of where data went |
| `outcome.refusals[].kind` | 27 kinds, of calls and of waits | 31: four more, of a capability request that was not sent |
| `outcome.lost` | No such field | What the record does not know |
| `asked.action`, `asked.version`, `asked.config`, `time.start_ms` | Never null | Null where `outcome.lost` names their group, and nowhere else |

The three fixed vectors are an ordinary root with one of each new element, a root a
dead process left, and a run whose admission could not be read
(`record3::the_three_fixed_vectors_are_read_written_back_and_valid`).

### A setting

`asked.settings` lists the resolved settings the run ran under. An element is **a name
and a value, written as an argument is**: `{"name": …, "value": {"text": …}}`, the value
under the name of its kind (`number`, `integer`, `boolean`, `text`).

- **The order is stated:** the order of the names' bytes, each name once.
- A name is 1 to 128 printable ASCII characters with no space, no `"` and no `\`
  (`SETTING_NAME_LIMIT`). A text is at most 256 characters (`SETTING_TEXT_LIMIT`), which
  the reader and the schema hold as the constructor does: a setting's value has a
  definition of its own in the schema, and an argument's text has no such bound. A
  record lists at most 96 settings (`SETTINGS_LIMIT`).
- **A number is written as it is.** Nothing is taken out of a number or an integer. One
  that is not finite cannot be written, and one whose digits, as they are written,
  hold a secret the executor was told of would be written with it: neither makes a
  setting.
- What the schema cannot say of a setting, and the types hold: the characters of a
  name, the order of the names, and a name once. The validator of the test kit knows
  no keyword for the characters of a text
  (`record3::what_the_schema_cannot_say_of_a_setting_the_types_refuse`).
- **A value passes through redaction before it is written.** `Setting::new` takes the
  executor's secrets: a text has them taken out while it is whole and is then cut; a
  name that holds a secret, or is not a name, makes no setting
  (`facts::a_settings_value_is_redacted_whole_and_then_cut_and_a_name_with_a_secret_makes_none`).

(`record3::a_setting_is_a_name_and_a_value_in_the_order_of_the_names`.)

### A paid request

`spend.requests` lists the run's paid requests, in the order they were sent. Every
amount is in millionths of a US dollar and is under the largest amount, which is none
(ADR 0007).

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| `request` | Its place in `models`, from 0: which request this is |
| `reserved_micros` | What was held for it: its bound (ADR 0010) |
| `settled_micros` | What it was settled at |
| `unresolved_micros` | How much of that no answer settled |
| `stage` | The stage it reached: `not_sent`, `answered` or `unresolved` (ADR 0010) |
| `over_bound` | Whether it was settled at more than was held |

What the amounts must be for each stage, which the types hold and the schema cannot
say:

| Stage | `settled_micros` | `unresolved_micros` |
|---|---|---|
| `not_sent` | 0 | 0 |
| `answered` (a response, or a fault that an answer settled) | The charge the answer gave, or the bound when it gave none | 0 |
| `unresolved` | The bound | The bound |

`over_bound` is true exactly when `settled_micros` is over `reserved_micros`. Each
request is named once, in order, and is one the record lists. A record lists at most
1,024 (`PAID_LIMIT`), which is the most requests a run sends. There is one constructor
for each stage (`Paid::not_sent`, `Paid::answered`, `Paid::unresolved`), and none makes
a paid request the reader refuses
(`record3::a_paid_request_is_one_the_run_sent_with_what_it_held_and_what_it_cost`,
`record3::what_the_schema_cannot_say_of_a_paid_request_the_types_refuse`,
`facts::a_paid_request_is_made_for_each_stage_and_never_one_the_reader_refuses`).

A paid request that was refused before it was dispatched is not here: nothing was held
for it. It is a refusal, below.

### Where data went

`egress` lists where the run's data went, in the order each entry was first noted.

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| `origin` | The origin that was reached, or that a send was refused for, as an origin is written (ADR 0012): `scheme://host`, with the port when it is not the scheme's own |
| `account` | The account used with it, **cut** as it is always shown (`Account::shown`): its first four characters, and `...` when there were more. Null when there is none |
| `classes` | The classes of data, in the order `source_images`, `derived_images`, `prompts`, `evidence`, each once |
| `outcome` | `sent`, or `refused` |

Each origin, account and outcome is listed once; noted again, it gains the classes of
the new note. A record lists at most 128 entries (`EGRESS_LIMIT`). **An account passes
through redaction before it is written:** the whole account is looked at for a secret
the executor was told of, and one that holds any is not written at all, not even cut;
the entry then names no account. Any other is written cut
(`record3::where_data_went_is_an_origin_a_cut_account_the_classes_and_whether_it_was_sent`,
`record3::what_the_schema_cannot_say_of_where_data_went_the_types_refuse`,
`facts::where_data_went_is_noted_once_for_each_recipient_and_outcome_with_a_cut_account`).

### A refusal of a capability request

`outcome.refusals` (ADR 0008) listed the calls a run made that were refused, and a wait
that was refused. It gains the kinds of **a capability request that was not sent**. Such
a refusal names no action: `action` is null.

| Kind | The request was not sent because |
|---|---|
| `request_no_bound` | Its backend states no enforceable bound |
| `request_over_allowance` | Its bound is over what its run has left |
| `request_not_approved` | Its recipient is not approved for the classes of data it holds |
| `request_not_ready` | Its model is not installed, or is being removed |

The schema holds this too: an element of the list is one of two shapes, a refusal of a
call or a wait, which may name an action, or a refusal of a request, whose action is
null. `Refused::of_a_request` makes one, its sentence redacted and cut as every refusal's is.
They share the list's bound of 256 with the others
(`record3::a_refusal_of_a_capability_request_names_no_action`,
`facts::a_refusal_of_a_request_is_made_only_of_a_kind_that_is_one`).

### What is lost

`outcome.lost` names every group the record does not know. **For a run that ended by
itself it is empty.** A run that a dead process left is ended as an error of the kind
`interrupted`, with a record made from its admission alone; everything else is lost, by
the plan's decision that recovery reconstructs nothing. So `outcome.lost` is one of
three lists, and no other:

| The run | `outcome.lost` |
|---|---|
| Ended by itself | `[]` |
| Left by a dead process, its admission read | The twelve groups below, in this order |
| The same, its admission not readable | Those twelve, and then `admission` |

**The mapping of fields to groups:**

| Group | Fields | When the group is lost |
|---|---|---|
| `arguments` | `asked.arguments` | Empty |
| `settings` | `asked.settings`; `asked.config` | Empty; null |
| `inputs` | `inputs` | Empty |
| `refusals` | `outcome.refusals`; `outcome.refusals_omitted` | Empty; 0 |
| `durations` | `time.durations` | Empty |
| `models` | `models` | Empty |
| `requests` | `spend.requests` | Empty |
| `egress` | `egress` | Empty |
| `steps` | `steps` | Empty |
| `judge` | `judge` | Empty |
| `diagnostics` | `diagnostics`, the journal among them | Empty |
| `version` | `identity.whizbang` | It holds the version that ended the run; the version that ran it is not known |
| `admission` | `identity.request`, `identity.parent`, `asked.action`, `asked.version`, `time.start_ms`, `spend.reserved_micros` | Each null |

`outputs` is no group: only a completed run has outputs, and nothing is published
before a run ends.

The rules, each held by the types and by the schema:

- **`outcome.lost` with anything in it is refused unless the status is `error` and the
  kind `interrupted`**
  (`record3::lost_with_anything_in_it_is_refused_unless_the_run_is_an_interrupted_error`).
  The other way round does not hold: an action may end with an error it calls
  `interrupted` itself, and nothing of that run is lost.
- **It is all of it, in one order,** with the admission or without: a list that leaves a
  group out, names one twice or orders them otherwise is refused
  (`record3::what_is_lost_is_all_of_it_in_one_order_with_the_admission_or_without`).
- **A field that is null only because its group was lost is refused where
  `outcome.lost` does not name that group.** `asked.action`, `asked.version`,
  `asked.config` and `time.start_ms` are never null in a run that ended by itself; and
  with the admission read, what the admission gives is there
  (`record3::a_field_that_is_null_only_because_its_group_was_lost_is_refused_where_lost_does_not_name_it`).
- **A field whose group is lost holds nothing**
  (`record3::a_field_whose_group_is_lost_holds_nothing`).
- **A null that says something is known to be absent is as before:** a root has no
  parent, a request may have no id, and an executor with no ledger reserved nothing.
  With the admission read these three are whatever the admission says.

**Readers key on `outcome.lost`, never on the error's kind.** An interrupted record's
`spend.requests` is `[]`, as its other lists are, and only `outcome.lost` says that this
means "not known" and not "none": a reader that shows or counts a list looks there
first. The binary's `runs show` prints `unknown` for every group that is lost, the
paid requests and the models among them, and what was reserved when the admission is
lost too, so that an interrupted run never reads as one that spent nothing; and it
prints the settings, the paid requests and where data went of a run that has them
(`runs::runs_show_says_unknown_for_every_group_a_record_has_lost_and_never_none`,
`runs::runs_show_prints_the_settings_the_paid_requests_and_where_data_went`,
`runs::runs_show_prints_a_run_2_record_as_it_did`).

`RunRecord::interrupted` makes such a record, from the run's name, what its admission
said when it could be read, the version that ends it and the time. What an admission
said was read from a store, so the record is made only when it is one its own reader
takes: an empty version or action, a parent that is the run itself, or the largest
amount as what was reserved, makes none
(`record::facts::a_record_of_a_run_a_dead_process_left_that_its_reader_would_refuse_is_not_made`)
(`facts::the_record_of_a_run_a_dead_process_left_is_the_fixed_vectors`). Who calls it,
and what readers say of such a run, is task I4b's.

### The schema

`run-3.json` is one of three shapes (`oneOf`): a run that ended by itself, a run a dead
process left, and one whose admission could not be read. Each of the last two fixes
`outcome.lost` to its list, the status to `error`, the error's kind to `interrupted`,
and every lost field to empty or null. The validator of the test kit knows no keyword
that says "if this, then that", and three shapes that exclude each other say the same.

**The schema and the types refuse the same faults,** one document for each, in ADR
0005's three ways: a document changed, held to both (`record3::…`); what the schema
cannot say and the types still refuse, named as that (`record3::what_the_schema_cannot_say_…`);
and the tests of ADR 0005 themselves, which run on `run/3`
(`record::what_is_not_a_record_is_refused_by_the_types_and_by_the_schema`).

### What reading does with a `run/2` record

**It is read, as frozen at `m1a`.** `RunRecord::from_bytes` looks at the version first:
`run/3` is read as above; `run/2` is read by a reader of its own, which holds the
shape the tag wrote, with the three lists unable to hold anything, no `outcome.lost`,
no refusal of a request and nothing null that `run/2` did not let be null. It is written
back as the bytes it was, without the field it has not. `run-2.json` is kept as it was
(`RUN_2_SCHEMA`). Any other version is another version, as before
(`record3::a_run_2_record_written_by_the_tag_is_read_and_written_back_as_it_was`,
`record3::a_run_2_record_holds_nothing_that_run_3_added`,
`record3::a_record_of_a_version_this_build_does_not_read_is_another_version`).

Nothing writes `run/2` again, and nothing migrates one: a store holds the records it
was given.

### Bounds, and the sum

Each new list is bounded where it comes in: `Facts`, which is what a record takes of
settings, paid requests and where data went, takes no more than a record lists, and
says so to whoever notes one more
(`facts::each_new_list_takes_no_more_than_a_record_lists`).

With every new list full and every field at its longest, the three lists come to
under 420,000 bytes: 96 settings of a name of 128 and a text of 256 characters of six
bytes each; 1,024 paid requests of at most 174 bytes; 128 entries of where data went of
at most 420 bytes. ADR 0008 left 900,000 bytes of a record's 4 MiB for what it did not
count. A record made with the three lists full is measured, and the sum with ADR 0008's
is under the limit
(`facts::a_record_with_every_new_list_full_is_under_the_limit_with_the_rest_at_its_worst`).

### A run's place for facts

Added by task B4b. The code is `crates/whizbang-core/src/contract/facts.rs`, with the
executor's part in `engine.rs`; the tests are
`crates/whizbang-core/tests/it/contract/facts.rs`, named below as `facts::…`.

The executor does not know what a setting resolved to, what a request cost or where
data went: the config, the ledger and the gate do, and each is another module. **The
executor gives each run a place, `RunFacts`, where they say so while the run executes,
and writes what was noted when the run ends.** An action's context hands it out
(`Context::facts`); a clone is the same place.

| Noted with | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `RunFacts::setting(name, value)` | An element of `asked.settings` |
| `RunFacts::paid(paid)` | An element of `spend.requests` |
| `RunFacts::went(origin, account, classes, outcome)` | An element of `egress`, or more classes on one that is listed |
| `RunFacts::refused(kind, why)` | An element of `outcome.refusals`, of a kind that is of a request |

- **Each note answers whether it was taken.** One is not taken when the record lists
  as many as it may, when it is not one a record can hold (a name that is none, a kind
  that is of a call), and when the run's end has begun.
- **A fact of each kind is in the run's record however the run ended:** completed,
  failed, cancelled, or panicked
  (`facts::a_fact_of_each_kind_noted_inside_a_run_is_in_its_record_however_the_run_ended`).
- **A refusal of a request goes with the run's other refusals,** in the order they were
  refused and within ADR 0008's bound of 256: past it, it is counted and not listed
  (`facts::a_refusal_of_a_request_is_in_the_runs_refusals_within_their_bound`).
- **A text is redacted when it is noted,** with the executor's secrets: a setting's
  value, a refusal's sentence, and an account, which is not written at all when it
  holds one. A setting whose name holds a secret is not taken
  (`facts::an_account_and_a_settings_value_are_redacted_when_they_are_noted`).
- **A note made after the run's end began is dropped, and changes no record.** The end
  begins, for what is noted, where the executor takes what was noted to write the
  record: the look and the note are one step under the place's lock, so a note is in
  the record or was answered no
  (`facts::a_note_made_after_the_runs_end_began_is_dropped_and_changes_no_record`).
- **Nothing in it is durable before the run's end.** What was noted is in memory until
  the one publication that ends the run. A process that dies first leaves a run whose
  record names these groups in `outcome.lost`, which is what recovery is to write
  (task I4b).
- **A run that fills every new list to its bound has a record under the limit:** 96
  settings, 1,024 paid requests for 1,024 requests it sent, and 128 entries of where
  data went, each at its longest, come to a record that is read back, under a quarter
  of the limit; and one more of each is not taken
  (`facts::a_run_that_fills_every_new_list_to_its_bound_has_a_record_under_the_limit`).
- **With the limit set low** (`Engine::with_record_limit`, the executor's seam for
  tests), a run whose record would be over it is not written as it is: it ends as an
  error of the type `record`, as ADR 0009 has it, and its record leaves out what was
  noted of the run with the requests, saying how many there were of each
  (`facts::with_the_limit_set_low_a_run_whose_facts_take_its_record_over_ends_as_an_error_that_lists_none`).

Limits: a context that other code made, as a test makes one, has a place that belongs
to no run, where a note is kept nowhere and a refusal is not taken. A paid request
noted for a request the run did not send is taken and then left out of the record,
since only the record knows which requests it lists. Who notes what is the strands':
nothing in the build notes a fact yet but a test.

### Limits, each pinned or stated

- **Nothing in the build fills the new lists yet.** A run has its place for facts ("A
  run's place for facts"), and no module notes anything in it but a test: the config,
  the ledger and the gate are the strands' (tasks C5, S10 and E4).
- **`asked.action`, `asked.version`, `asked.config` and `time.start_ms` are optional in
  the types,** for every reader, though only an interrupted record leaves one empty.
  A reader that wants them writes what it does when they are unknown.
- **`runs list` shows an unknown action and start as `unknown`** and says nothing more
  of an interrupted run. What the other readers say of one (`status`, `wait`,
  `cancel`, a repeat of its request) is task I4b's.
- **An account of four characters or fewer is written whole,** whatever its
  characters: three dots is such an account. A cut one is exactly seven characters
  that end in three dots, and the reader tells the two apart by length. That is the
  cut ADR 0012 decided.
- **The count of each new list is looked at before its entries are compared,** so a
  record far over a bound is refused at once
  (`record3::a_list_far_over_its_bound_is_refused_for_its_count_before_its_entries_are_compared`).
- **The largest amount cannot be refused by the schema,** which holds a number to a
  bound as a float; the types refuse it, as for `spend.reserved_micros` since ADR 0007.

## Consequences

- A store that holds a `run/3` record is not read by a build of the tag `m1a`. Before
  the tag `m1b` no store written by 1B is worth keeping (the plan, "What undoing each
  task takes").
- The strands fill three lists whose shape, order and bounds are fixed: a shape one of
  them shows to be wrong is `run/4`, never an edit.
- A reader of a record handles an action, a version, a config and a start that are
  unknown.
- The schema is three shapes, and about three times the size of `run-2.json`.

## Alternatives

- **`outcome.lost` as any subset of the groups.** Turned down: recovery loses all of it
  by decision, a subset would promise a fuller record nobody builds, and the schema
  could not hold a field to its group without a conditional.
- **Keep the lost fields as empty strings and zeros.** Turned down by the plan: a field
  such a group would fill is null or empty, and an empty action is not an action.
- **Refuse `run/2`,** as `run/2` refused `run/1`. Turned down by the plan: a `run/2`
  record written by the tag is read and shown.
- **Read `run/2` through the `run/3` types,** treating the missing field as empty.
  Turned down: a `run/2` record could then hold a setting or a null start, which the
  tag never wrote and its schema refuses.
- **The stage of a paid request as two booleans.** Turned down: the three stages are
  ADR 0010's, and a record spells them as a fault does.
- **A whole account in the record.** Turned down by ADR 0012 and the plan: it is shown
  cut everywhere.
